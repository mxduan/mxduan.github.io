      What this proxy can support

      Only one first stop may be removed, with unchanged assigned service identity and path and the expected later stops preserved. Failures and restart/source gaps split continuous segments. Identity/path changes, missing or ambiguous records, terminal boundaries and whole-trip disappearance cannot become confirmed departures. Relevant unknown transitions block headway pairs. Overlapping intervals leave order uncertain. No pair spans a gap, segment or New York day fragment.

      The vehicle clock and revised forecasts are not independent physical timing evidence. Internal feed lag, skipped stops and unseen trains can differ from physical service. Actual lateness remains unavailable; no on-time percentage or global punctuality rank is inferred. Each example is a dated observation, even when the public snapshot is stale.

      Model, identity and missing coverage

      No on-time rate is calculated. Zero observed flags does not establish zero physical delays. Already-overdue first observations and missing, unassigned or ambiguous records cannot be classified. A disappearance or feed gap does not prove recovery or cancellation. Stops and predictions are operator estimates; model accuracy has not been independently validated.

      Raw evidence stays private. Collection pauses at its disk ceiling; raw records are retained without automatic deletion. Historical summaries aim every 15 minutes, with a separate rider snapshot every minute while the same host is awake and online. Rider estimates expire after two minutes. A stale public snapshot does not establish the collector's current health.

      What counts as a delay event?

      We split affected routes and statuses on “|” and match route E exactly, within NYCT Subway. Delay labels are delays, some-delays, severe-delays and delays-and-cancellations. We count each event_id once, in the Monday–Sunday New York week of its first qualifying E-line delay update across the full available archive. Multi-line events count if E is affected.

      Planned work and other disruptions are excluded from this count. The archive does not establish actual train delays, incident duration or the number of affected passengers. Missing alerts and reporting changes can affect counts.

      Coverage, timing and refresh

      Archive-window means the entire calendar week is before the last available subway record; it does not guarantee complete reporting. Partial weeks are lower bounds. Unavailable weeks are never shown as zero.

      Current alerts are downloaded and cached on a non-MTA server. We use source timestamps, active periods and MTA Mercury alert categories and route priorities. Snapshots older than one minute are labeled stale. Refresh jobs aim for a snapshot every 15 minutes, on a best-effort schedule; historical and monthly data are checked at most daily. Jobs may be late or fail; reload checks our cache, not MTA directly. An alert-free snapshot does not prove that every train is on time.

      No link is assumed between current feed IDs and archive event IDs. Current polling observations are separate from the historical weekly metric.

      How this differs from Subway Now

      Subway Now estimates operational delays using train-feed progress, retained predictions and travel-time comparisons. Its delay flag is an inferred state, not a count of official alert events or independently confirmed timetable lateness. Its published backend documentation describes frequent processing and rolling travel-time estimates. Our weekly measure counts official delay-alert events. Fifteen-minute alert snapshots cannot establish individual train lateness, headway gaps or a complete delay history. The E/F operational-estimates view now uses a separate versioned observer with thresholds, artifact exclusions and partial collection coverage. It remains distinct from official alerts and verified physical lateness.

      Subway Data NYC offers a daily historical stop-time archive, but the times are reconstructed predictions. A disappearing trip can leave unvisited stops marked past, and missing trains do not establish cancellations. The reported-progress view now uses bounded stop-list transitions with explicit cohorts and coverage; this is separate from independently measured departure headways or train lateness.

      Monthly journey reliability

      Customer Journey Time Performance = 1 − sum(over_five_mins) / sum(num_passengers). Extra platform wait and extra onboard time use total_apt and total_att divided by modeled passengers. Peak and off-peak rows are passenger-weighted together, never averaged as percentages. These are modeled weekday single-leg metrics, not a weekly on-time train ranking.
