What counts as a delay event?

We split affected routes and statuses on “|” and match route E exactly, within NYCT Subway. Delay labels are delays, some-delays, severe-delays and delays-and-cancellations. We count each event_id once, in the Monday–Sunday New York week of its first qualifying E-line delay update across the full available archive. Multi-line events count if E is affected.

Planned work and other disruptions are excluded from this count. The archive does not establish actual train delays, incident duration or the number of affected passengers. Missing alerts and reporting changes can affect counts.

Coverage, timing and refresh

Archive-window means the entire calendar week is before the last available subway record; it does not guarantee complete reporting. Partial weeks are lower bounds. Unavailable weeks are never shown as zero.

Current alerts are downloaded and cached on a non-MTA server. We use source timestamps, active periods and MTA Mercury alert categories and route priorities. Snapshots older than one minute are labeled stale. Refresh jobs aim for a snapshot every 15 minutes, on a best-effort schedule; historical and monthly data are checked at most daily. Jobs may be late or fail; reload checks our cache, not MTA directly. An alert-free snapshot does not prove that every train is on time.

No link is assumed between current feed IDs and archive event IDs. Current polling observations are separate from the historical weekly metric.

How this differs from Subway Now

Subway Now estimates operational delays using train-feed progress, retained predictions and travel-time comparisons. Its delay flag is an inferred state, not a count of official alert events or independently confirmed timetable lateness. Its published backend documentation describes frequent processing and rolling travel-time estimates. Our weekly measure counts official delay-alert events. Fifteen-minute alert snapshots cannot establish individual train lateness, headway gaps or a complete delay history. Those would require a separate, validated observation pipeline with a stated threshold and coverage.

Subway Data NYC offers a daily historical stop-time archive, but the times are reconstructed predictions. A disappearing trip can leave unvisited stops marked past, and missing trains do not establish cancellations. We are validating transition evidence, local-day boundaries and coverage before publishing an estimated headway or train-level delay metric.

Monthly journey reliability

Customer Journey Time Performance = 1 − sum(over_five_mins) / sum(num_passengers). Extra platform wait and extra onboard time use total_apt and total_att divided by modeled passengers. Peak and off-peak rows are passenger-weighted together, never averaged as percentages. These are modeled weekday single-leg metrics, not a weekly on-time train ranking.
